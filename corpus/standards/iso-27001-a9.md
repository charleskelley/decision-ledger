# ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A.9 — Access Control

*SYNTHETIC ADAPTATION — This document is a synthetic adaptation of ISO/IEC 27001:2022
Annex A access control controls for the DecisionLedger reference implementation.
ISO/IEC 27001 is a proprietary international standard. This adaptation is not a
verbatim reproduction.*

## 1. A.9.1 — Access Control Policy

An access control policy must be established, documented, and reviewed based on
business and information security requirements. The policy must address:

- The access control rules, access rights, and restrictions for specific user roles.
- The mechanisms for authentication and authorisation.
- The requirements for formal access authorisation.
- Requirements for periodic review of access rights.

For online authentication systems, the access control policy must explicitly address
risk-based authentication: the principle that authentication requirements should be
proportionate to the risk presented by the access attempt, rather than fixed for
all access regardless of context.

## 2. A.9.2 — User Access Management

Procedures must be implemented to control the allocation of access rights. Specific
controls include:

- **A.9.2.1 User registration and deregistration**: A formal process for granting
  and revoking access. For online accounts, account creation and closure procedures
  must ensure that access rights are appropriately controlled throughout the account
  lifecycle.
- **A.9.2.4 Management of secret authentication information**: Allocation of secret
  authentication information must be controlled through a formal management process.
  Secret information must be protected from inappropriate disclosure.
- **A.9.2.5 Review of user access rights**: Access rights must be reviewed at regular
  intervals and upon any change in employment or role.

## 3. A.9.3 — User Responsibilities

Users must be made aware of their responsibilities for maintaining effective access
controls. Relevant to authentication risk systems, users must be informed of:

- The steps they should take if they suspect their credentials have been compromised.
- The actions the institution may take in response to detected anomalies (step-up
  authentication, session suspension, account lock).
- How to respond to account suspension notices and verify their own identity to
  restore access.

## 4. A.9.4 — System and Application Access Control

Access to systems and applications must be controlled. Controls include:

- **A.9.4.2 Secure log-on procedures**: Access to systems must be controlled by a
  secure log-on procedure. The log-on procedure must limit the number of invalid
  log-on attempts and log successful and unsuccessful attempts.
- **A.9.4.3 Password management system**: Password management systems must enforce
  minimum quality standards and detect repeated unsuccessful attempts to gain access.
- **A.9.4.4 Use of privileged utility programs**: Use of utility programs capable of
  overriding system and application controls must be restricted and tightly controlled.

For online banking, A.9.4.2 applies directly: the secure log-on procedure must detect
anomalous access patterns (impossible travel, new device, velocity spike) and apply
risk-appropriate controls, not merely validate the credential.

## 5. A.9.4.5 — Access Control to Program Source Code

Access to program source code must be restricted to prevent unauthorised modification.
This control is cited for its relevance to the ATO Reasoner pipeline: the prompt
templates (INT-POLICY-GATE-V1, §6) are immutable once deployed and changes require a
new version — a version control principle consistent with A.9.4.5 applied to policy
decision logic.

## 6. Continuous Improvement for Access Control

ISO 27001 requires that the information security management system be continually
improved. For access control, this means:

- Regular review of access control thresholds against current threat data.
- Post-incident analysis of detection failures and control gaps.
- Integration of threat intelligence to update access control policies.
- Measurement of control effectiveness through security testing and audit.

The eval harness dimensions in DecisionLedger implement this continuous improvement
requirement for the automated policy gate component: each release candidate is tested
against defined performance thresholds, and regressions block deployment.
